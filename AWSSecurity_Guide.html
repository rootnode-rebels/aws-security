1. What is this Project & Why Do We Need It?

Most websites today rely on just a password. But in the real world, hackers steal millions of passwords every year through fake phishing emails, big company data leaks, and computer viruses.

The Big Problem: When a hacker gets your real password, normal websites cannot tell if it is really you or a hacker! If someone enters the correct password, normal websites open the door and let them right in.

Our Solution (Behavior-Based Security): Instead of blindly trusting a password, our system looks at how, when, and where the login is happening:

  • If you log in from New York at 1:00 PM, and 10 minutes later someone uses your password from Tokyo, the system knows that no human can fly 8,500 km/h!
  • It instantly blocks the hacker, freezes the account, sounds a loud alarm siren on your real computer screen, and sends an emergency email with a verification code.
1. Airplane Speed Check
Calculates travel speed between logins. If it is faster than a passenger plane (over 900 km/h), access is blocked immediately.
2. Master Computer Lock
Your trusted personal laptop is your boss device. No stranger's computer or new phone can log in without your direct approval.
3. Loud Alarm Siren
When a hacker is blocked, your screen flashes bright red and plays a loud emergency siren so you can stop it right away.

3. How the Cloud Services Work (Explained in Simple English)

Here is how the entire system works from the moment someone types their password to the moment an attack is stopped:

System Architecture: Complete Step-by-Step Flow

What Each Step Does in Plain English:

Step 1: Web Screens: A real user or a hacker types an email and password into their browser.
Step 2: Amazon Front Door (API Gateway): Welcomes the visitor, finds out what city and country they are connecting from, and sends the request to the cloud.
Step 3: Amazon Cloud Workers (AWS Lambda): Safely verifies the password, checks how fast the user moved, and calculates a Safety Score from 0 to 100.
Step 4: The Security Guard (Amazon CloudWatch): If it is a hacker, CloudWatch counts it. If 5 attacks happen within 1 minute, an automated emergency alarm sounds!
Step 5: Alerts & Database: Blocks the hacker with a 403 Forbidden error, sounds a loud siren on the real user's laptop, and sends an alert email.

4. The 7 Clues Used to Catch Hackers

Every single time someone tries to log in, our system checks 7 simple clues to determine if it is really you or an impostor:

Clue / Check What Does It Look For? When Does It Ring the Alarm? Weight
1. Travel Speed How fast did the user move between their previous login and this login? Moving faster than an airplane (over 900 km/h) 40%
2. Distance from Home Is the login happening far away from the user's usual home city? More than 1,000 km away from home 20%
3. Computer Match Is this the user's usual computer screen, operating system, and browser? Completely unfamiliar computer 15%
4. Hacker IP Check Is the login coming from a hacker VPN, Tor network, or cloud server farm? Known hacker VPN or cloud datacenter 15%
5. Wrong Password Spike Did someone just type 3 or more incorrect passwords in a row? 3 or more failed password attempts 10%
6. Unusual Time of Day Is someone logging in at 3:00 AM when the user only logs in during the afternoon? Unusual middle-of-the-night login 5%
7. Robot Script Check Is this a real human typing or an automated computer script? Automated hacker robot detected 5%

The 3 AI Models in Simple Terms

  1. Model 1 (The Known Attack Spotter): Recognizes common hacker tricks like typing long lists of stolen passwords very quickly.
  2. Model 2 (The Weird Behavior Spotter): Catches brand-new, strange behavior that does not look like anything a normal person would do.
  3. Model 3 (The Daily Routine Learner): Learns what your normal daily schedule looks like and rings an alarm if multiple things change all at once.
  4. Clear Reasons for Every Block: The system always tells you the exact reason why someone was blocked (for example: "+45 points: Impossible Travel at 8,500 km/h").

5. What Happens Based on the Score?

The system adds up all the clues into a single Score from 0 to 100:

MEDIUM RISK 40 to 69 pts VERIFY CODE Quick Extra Check: Something looks a little unusual (like using a new laptop in your same town). A 6-digit code is sent to your trusted computer.
HIGH RISK 70 to 100 pts BLOCK HACKER Attack Blocked: Immediate 403 Forbidden rejection. The account is frozen. A loud siren sounds on your real laptop, a red banner drops down, and an emergency email is sent.

9. Viva Voce & Exam Questions (Simple Answers)

Here are the 5 most common questions examiners ask, with simple, direct answers you can speak out loud:

Q1: Why do we need this system if a user already has a strong password?
Answer: Because passwords get stolen every day through phishing, computer viruses, and data breaches. When a hacker gets your password, a normal website lets them right in because the password is correct! Our system checks behavior (where you are, how fast you moved, and what device you are using) so it can catch and block a hacker even when they have your real password.
Q2: How does the system calculate "Impossible Travel"?
Answer: It checks the distance between your last login and your new login, and divides it by the time passed. If you logged in from New York at 1:00 PM and someone logs in from Tokyo at 1:10 PM, that requires a travel speed of over 8,500 km/h. Since commercial passenger airplanes only fly at 900 km/h, this is physically impossible for a human, so the system blocks it immediately.
Q3: What is "Primary Device Authority" and why is it important?
Answer: Your trusted personal laptop is registered as your "Master Computer". If a hacker tries to log in from an unknown phone or computer, they cannot touch your account controls. Instead, an emergency alert and a 6-digit approval code appear only on your master computer. Without your physical approval from your screen, the hacker cannot get in.
Q4: What is an "Anti-Timing Attack" and how do we prevent it?
Answer: Hackers measure how many milliseconds a server takes to respond. If a server responds quickly when an email does not exist, but takes longer when an email does exist, the hacker can figure out which emails belong to real users. We prevent this by running the exact same 200,000-round password calculation even when an email doesn't exist, making response times completely identical.
Q5: What role does Amazon CloudWatch play in this project?
Answer: CloudWatch is our 24/7 security watchtower. It counts every blocked attack in real time. If more than 5 attacks happen in 1 minute, CloudWatch automatically triggers a high-severity alarm that broadcasts emergency notifications via Amazon SNS so security teams can respond immediately.
